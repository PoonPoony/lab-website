A multi-layer interior permanent magnet synchronous motor (IPMSM) offers high power density, with increased saliency ratio and reluctance torque achieved by adjusting the number of permanent magnet (PM) layers. However, this also raises induced voltage harmonics, which can exceed the DC-link voltage, leading to unstable control. To ensure stable operation, the induced voltage must remain within the DC-link voltage. This paper proposes a design method to analyze and mitigate the significant harmonic components by decomposing the induced voltage. Using 2D finite element analysis (FEA) with a fixed permeability method, the induced voltage is separated, and its main harmonic components are identified. A design strategy is then introduced to reduce harmonic magnitude by optimizing the number of slots and applying rotor skew and eccentricity. The proposed method is validated through both simulations and experiments conducted on a prototype.
