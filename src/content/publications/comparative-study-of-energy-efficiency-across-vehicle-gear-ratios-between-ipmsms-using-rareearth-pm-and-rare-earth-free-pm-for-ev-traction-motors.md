This article compares the vehicle energy efficiency of rare-earth (RE) and rare-earth-free (REF) interior permanent magnet synchronous motors (IPMSMs) with identical outer dimensions and nominal ratings under a standardized driving cycle. Both models were designed for comparable back-EMF, and copper, iron, and PM eddy current losses were analyzed. A forward vehicle model evaluated the Common Artemis Driving Cycle (CADC) across different gear ratios (GR). The REF motor exhibited larger copper loss at low speed, while the RE motor incurred higher iron loss at high speed. Although PM eddy current loss was a minor portion of total losses, its difference between models was comparable to other loss components and thus contributed meaningfully to vehicle energy efficiency. Overall, the RE motor achieved higher efficiency, but the gap narrowed with increasing GR, suggesting that appropriate GR selection can enable REF-based motors to approach competitive performance in EV traction.
