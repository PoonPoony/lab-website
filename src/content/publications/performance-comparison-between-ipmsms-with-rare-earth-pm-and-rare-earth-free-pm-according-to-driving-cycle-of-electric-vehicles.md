This paper presents a comparative analysis of two interior permanent magnet synchronous motors (IPMSMs) using rare-earth (RE) permanent magnet (PM) and rare-earth-free (REF) PM. Both motors are designed with identical outer geometries and power ratings to enable fair performance evaluation. The comparison focuses on electromagnetic losses and energy efficiency under standard driving cycles including UDDS for urban conditions and HWFET for highway conditions. Simulation results show that the RE model achieves lower copper losses due to stronger magnetic excitation. The REF model shows reduced iron loss due to smaller flux variation. PM eddy current losses are negligible in both models. Under the UDDS cycle, the RE model shows better efficiency due to lower current demand. In the HWFET cycle, the REF model achieves slightly higher efficiency due to reduced iron losses at high speeds. These results suggest that a well-designed REF-based motor can serve as a suitable alternative to RE-based designs. REF PM model may serve as a viable alternative to RE PM models in high-speed-dominant applications.
