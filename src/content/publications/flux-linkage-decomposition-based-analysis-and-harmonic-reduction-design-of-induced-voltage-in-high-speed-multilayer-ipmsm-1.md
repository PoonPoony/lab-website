A multilayer interior permanent magnet synchronous motor (IPMSM) offers high power density and improved reluctance torque through the adjustment of permanent magnet (PM) layers. However, at high-speed operation, these multilayer structures inherently generate significant induced voltage harmonics due to magnetic saturation, spatial flux variations, and PM segmentation effects. These harmonics can exceed the inverter DC-link voltage, leading to instability in motor control. In this paper, a systematic harmonic analysis and reduction method is proposed based on flux linkage decomposition. Using a 2D finite element analysis (FEA) combined with a frozen permeability approach, the total induced voltage is separated into three components: the time-differential of current, the spatial-differential of inductance, and the rotational variation of PM flux. Harmonic analysis of each component reveals the dominant contributions of the 11th and 13th orders. To mitigate these harmonics, three targeted design strategies are proposed: increasing the stator slot number, applying three-step rotor skewing, and introducing an eccentric rotor shape. These methods are implemented in an improved model, which is analyzed via FEA and verified through experimental tests. The results confirm a significant reduction in total harmonic distortion (THD) from 71.1% to 6.5%, and experimental errors in torque and power are within 1.5%, validating the proposed method for high-speed EV traction applications.
